Why the old answer is not enough

y = c1e(λ + iμ)t + c2e(λ − iμ)t

What does e to an imaginary power mean? And the answer should be a real function: a position, a voltage.

Two questions: what is eit, and how do we get real solutions?

↳ the first question has a one-page answer.

Euler’s formula, from Taylor series

ex = 1 + x + x22! + x33! + x44! + x55! + …  true for every number x

Put x = it

i2 = −1,  i3 = −i,  i4 = 1,  i5 = i

eit = 1 + it − t22! − it33! + t44! + it55! − …

Collect the terms without i, then the terms with i

= (1 − t22! + t44! − …) + i(t − t33! + t55! − …)

= cos t + i sin t  the Taylor series of cos and sin

eit = cos t + i sin t

t → −t:  e−it = cos t − i sin t  cos is even, sin is odd

So for our roots

e(λ + iμ)t = eλteiμt = eλt(cos μt + i sin μt)

e(λ − iμ)t = eλt(cos μt − i sin μt)

↳ the growth comes from λ, the turning from μ. Now make them real.

Two real solutions from two complex ones

y1 = eλt(cos μt + i sin μt),   y2 = eλt(cos μt − i sin μt)

Superposition, from Wednesday

Sums and constant multiples of solutions are solutions. The constants may be complex.

Add

y1 + y2 = 2eλt cos μt

u = y1 + y22 = eλt cos μt  real

Subtract

y1 − y2 = 2ieλt sin μt

v = y1 − y22i = eλt sin μt  real

The general solution

y = eλt(c1 cos μt + c2 sin μt)

Two real solutions, two constants: enough for two initial conditions.

The i has disappeared. We used it to find the answer; the answer does not contain it.

Only the root with + is needed: λ and μ are read from it.

↳ the recipe, with the derivative for initial conditions.

The recipe

ay″ + by′ + cy = 0,   b2 − 4ac < 0

1. Characteristic equation ar2 + br + c = 0.

2. Quadratic formula. Write the roots as λ ± iμ with μ > 0.

3. General solution y = eλt(c1 cos μt + c2 sin μt).

4. Initial conditions: put in t0, solve for c1, c2.

You can always check: λ = −b/(2a).

The derivative, by the product rule

y′ = λeλt(c1 cos μt + c2 sin μt)

    + eλt(−μc1 sin μt + μc2 cos μt)

At t = 0: cos 0 = 1, sin 0 = 0

y(0) = c1

y′(0) = λc1 + μc2

↳ first the simplest case, λ = 0.

Period 2π/3: the solution repeats forever. A spring with no friction.

Example 2 — a decaying oscillation

The solution starts at 2 with slope 8, then oscillates with period 2π/3 ≈ 2.1.

Dashed: ±√13e−t/2. The curve stays between them.

the size is set by eλt = e−t/2 → 0

λ < 0: oscillations that die out.

↳ what happens for other signs of λ.

The sign of λ decides the long run

λ < 0 (teal): eλt → 0. The oscillation dies out.

λ = 0 (yellow): the same size forever.

λ > 0 (magenta): eλt → ∞. The oscillation grows.

λ = −b/(2a): only the sign of b/a matters

μ sets how fast it turns: the period is 2π/μ.
