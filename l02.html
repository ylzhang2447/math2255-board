Quick review

Order

The order of the highest derivative in the equation.

Linear

y and its derivatives appear by themselves.

not linear:   y2,   sin y,   y y′

ODE

One independent variable, one unknown function. Every equation in this course.

Quick review

Solution

A function that makes the equation true.

General solution

The solution that still has a c in it. It describes all of them at once.

Initial condition

One value, like y(0) = 5. It picks out one c.

General solution, then initial condition, then solve for c.

What does a solution look like?

Last time we learned to classify equations. Today we draw a solution without solving anything.

The falling object, with numbers

m = 10 kg,   γ = 2 kg/s,   g = 9.8 m/s2

dvdt = 9.8 − v5

We cannot solve this yet. But we can still see the answer.

The equation gives you the slope

A solution is a curve v(t). At any point on that curve, its slope is dvdt.

And the equation tells you dvdt — before you know the curve.

↳ pick a point, compute the slope, draw a tiny segment there

Three points

at v = 0:   9.8 − 0 = 9.8  steep, rising

at v = 40:   9.8 − 8 = 1.8  gentle, still rising

at v = 60:   9.8 − 12 = −2.2  falling

Do that at enough points and the whole picture appears. That picture is called a direction field.

One shortcut that saves you all the work

Look at the right-hand side again.

dvdt = 9.8 − v5   ← no t anywhere

The slope depends only on how fast you are going, not on what time it is.

↳ so along any horizontal line, every segment has the same slope

Compute one number, then fill the entire row.

Activity 1 — draw the field

  1. Copy these axes into your notebook.
  2. For v = 0, 10, 20, … 70, compute  9.8 − v/5.
  3. At every dot in that row, draw a short segment with that slope.
  4. Then sketch the curve that starts at v(0) = 0.

12 minutes.  Work with the person next to you.

Here is what you should have

Every curve bends toward the same horizontal line.

Terminal velocity

Start slower than 49 and you speed up. Start faster and you slow down.

↳ either way you end up at the same place

v → 49 m/s

That is the terminal velocity of this object.

And notice what we just did: we read it off the picture, and we never solved the equation.

Equilibrium solutions

The line v = 49 is special. It is itself a solution — a constant one.

Check it

if v = 49   then   dvdt = 9.8 − 495 = 0 ✓

A constant solution is called an equilibrium solution.

↳ to find them, set the right-hand side to zero and solve

9.8 − v5 = 0   ⇒   v = 49

Activity 2 — find the equilibria

  1. Set the right-hand side equal to zero.
  2. Solve. There may be more than one, or none at all.
  3. Then say whether nearby solutions move toward it or away.

(a)  dydt = y − 3

(b)  dpdt = 0.5p − 450

(c)  dydt = y2 − 4

8 minutes.  Same pairs. Your own paper.

Activity 2 — answers

(a)  y − 3 = 0 ⇒ y = 3  solutions move away

(b)  0.5p − 450 = 0 ⇒ p = 900  solutions move away

(c)  y2 − 4 = 0 ⇒ y = 2 and y = −2

↳ near y = −2 they move toward; near y = 2 they move away

The falling object was the toward kind. That is why it had a terminal velocity.

Now let’s actually solve one

Last time I handed you this, and all we did was check it:

p(t) = 900 + cet/2

Today you are going to get it yourself.

The equation

dpdt = 0.5p − 450

↳ four steps, and you already know all the calculus you need

Activity 3 — solve it

  1. Factor 0.5 out of the right-hand side.
  2. Divide both sides by (p − 900), and multiply both sides by dt.
  3. Integrate both sides.
  4. Solve for p.

8 minutes.  The four steps stay on the board.

Stuck on step 3?   ∫ duu = ln|u| + C

Activity 3 — the answer

dpdt = 0.5(p − 900)

dpp − 900 = 0.5 dt

ln|p − 900| = 0.5t + C

p − 900 = ce0.5t

p(t) = 900 + ce0.5t

Where did the 900 come from?

It is the equilibrium — the same number you found in Activity 2.

↳ and a = 0.5 is positive, so the exponential grows

Solutions run away from 900. Exactly what the direction field would have shown you.
