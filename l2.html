Quick review

Order

The order of the highest derivative in the equation.

Linear

y and its derivatives appear by themselves.

not linear:   y2,   sin y,   y y′

ODE

One independent variable, one unknown function. Every equation in this course.

Quick review

Solution

A function that makes the equation true.

General solution

The solution that still has a c in it. It describes all of them at once.

Initial condition

One value, like y(0) = 5. It picks out one c.

General solution, then initial condition, then solve for c.

Six quick ones — order? linear?

(a)  y″ + 3y′ + 2y = cos t   2nd order, linear

(b)  dydt = y2 − 4   1st order, nonlinear

(c)  t2y′ + ety = ln t   1st order, linear

(d)  y‴ = y′ + sin y   3rd order, nonlinear

(e)  dpdt = 0.5p − 450   1st order, linear

(f)  y′y = t   1st order, nonlinear

↳ the coefficients can be as ugly as they like. Only the y decides it.

Direction fields

A solution is a curve y(t). At any point on it, the slope is dydt.

And the equation tells you that slope, before you know the curve.

↳ pick a point, compute the slope, draw a tiny segment there

Do that at enough points and the picture appears. That picture is the direction field.

You can see the answer without solving anything.

Example 1

dvdt = 9.8 − v5  a falling object

 v = 0 :   9.8 − 05 = 9.8

 v = 10:   9.8 − 105 = 7.8

 v = 20:   9.8 − 205 = 5.8

 v = 30:   9.8 − 305 = 3.8

 v = 40:   9.8 − 405 = 1.8

 v = 50:   9.8 − 505 = −0.2

 v = 60:   9.8 − 605 = −2.2

 v = 70:   9.8 − 705 = −4.2

↳ one number per row. No t on the right, so the whole row is the same.

Example 1 — what the solutions do

Five different starting speeds. They all end up in the same place.

v = 49  is the terminal velocity.

Example 2

dydt = y − 2

 y = −2:   −2 − 2 = −4

 y = −1:   −1 − 2 = −3

 y = 0:   0 − 2 = −2

 y = 1:   1 − 2 = −1

 y = 2:   2 − 2 = 0

 y = 3:   3 − 2 = 1

 y = 4:   4 − 2 = 2

 y = 5:   5 − 2 = 3

Same method, opposite behaviour. Solutions run away from 2.

2.  dydt = y + 1  use y = −3 … 3

Problem 1 — answer

dydt = 3 − y  slopes 3, 2, 1, 0, −1, −2, −3

Zero at y = 3. Solutions run toward it.

Problem 2 — answer

dydt = y + 1  slopes −2, −1, 0, 1, 2, 3, 4

Zero at y = −1. Solutions run away from it.

Problem 3 — answer

dydt = y(2 − y)  slopes −3, 0, 1, 0, −3

Two flat lines: y = 0 and y = 2.

Equilibrium solutions

Those flat lines are solutions too. Constant ones.

A constant solution is called an equilibrium solution.

To find them

Set the right-hand side to zero and solve.

To tell toward from away

Plug in a number just above, and one just below. Look at the sign.

Example 3

dvdt = 9.8 − v5

Find it

9.8 − v5 = 0   ⇒   v5 = 9.8   ⇒   v = 49

Just above

v = 50:   9.8 − 10 = −0.2  negative → comes back down

Just below

v = 40:   9.8 − 8 = 1.8  positive → climbs up

Both sides come back. v = 49 is toward.

Example 4 — a nonlinear one

dydt = y2 − 4

y2 − 4 = 0   ⇒   (y − 2)(y + 2) = 0   ⇒   y = 2 and y = −2

Test four numbers

y = 3:  9 − 4 = 5  up    y = 1:  1 − 4 = −3  down

y = −1:  1 − 4 = −3  down    y = −3:  9 − 4 = 5  up

↳ above 2 goes up, below 2 goes down  ⇒  y = 2 is away

↳ above −2 goes down, below −2 goes up  ⇒  y = −2 is toward

Problems 4 to 8 — answers

4.  2y − 6 = 0 ⇒ y = 3  test 4 → +2, test 2 → −2  ⇒  away

5.  0.5p − 450 = 0 ⇒ p = 900  test 1000 → +50  ⇒  away

6.  y = 3 away,  y = −3 toward  same shape as Example 4

7.  y2 + 1 = 0 has no real solution  no equilibrium at all

8.  y = 0 away,  y = 4 toward

None, one, or two. Always check.

Example 5 — solving one

dydt = y − 3  this was problem 4's cousin

Step 1  y on one side, t on the other

dyy − 3 = dt

Step 2  integrate both sides

ln|y − 3| = t + C

Step 3  raise e to both sides

y − 3 = cet

y(t) = 3 + cet

↳ the 3 is the equilibrium. It came back.

Example 6 — one with a coefficient

dpdt = 0.5p − 450  the field mice

Step 0  factor the 0.5 out first

0.5p − 450 = 0.5(p − 900)  450 ÷ 0.5 = 900

dpp − 900 = 0.5 dt

ln|p − 900| = 0.5t + C

p − 900 = ce0.5t

p(t) = 900 + ce0.5t

↳ 900 is the equilibrium again, and 0.5 is positive, so this one grows away

9.  dydt = y − 5

11.  dydt = 3 − y  careful with the sign

Stuck on the integral?   ∫ duu = ln|u| + C

Problems 9 to 11 — answers

9.  ln|y − 5| = t + C   ⇒   y = 5 + cet

10.  2y − 8 = 2(y − 4)   ⇒   y = 4 + ce2t

11.  −ln|3 − y| = t + C   ⇒   y = 3 + ce−t

Look at all three

y = (equilibrium) + c e(rate) t

↳ rate positive → runs away.  rate negative → comes toward.
