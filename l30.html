Equations whose right side switches on or off at some time c.

We use the method of §6.2, together with both directions from Friday.

No new formula today: only a new kind of problem.

The problem: a force that switches on or off

ay″ + by′ + cy = g(t),   g has jumps

teal: a force switched on at t = 2, u2(t).

magenta: a force of 2 that is on from t = 1 to t = 4, 2(u1(t) − u4(t)).

Undetermined coefficients works on one formula at a time. Here we would solve on each piece, and match y and y′ at every switch.

The Laplace transform handles all the pieces at once.

↳ the method.

The method

ℒ{y′} = sY − y(0),   ℒ{y″} = s2Y − sy(0) − y′(0)

1. Write g(t) with step functions.

2. Transform both sides. Each step in g gives a factor e−cs.

3. Solve for Y(s). Group the terms by their factor e−cs.

4. Partial fractions on each fraction, without the factor e−cs.

5. Invert each fraction. Then shift: replace t by t − c, multiply by uc(t).

You can always check: on each piece, y solves the equation, and y, y′ do not jump.

Example 1 — a first order equation

y′ + 2y = 4u3(t),   y(0) = 1

2. Transform both sides

sY − 1 + 2Y = 4e−3ss

3. Solve for Y

(s + 2)Y = 1 + 4e−3ss

Y = 1s + 2 + e−3s4s(s + 2)

4. Partial fractions, without e−3s

4s(s + 2) = 2s − 2s + 2  →  2 − 2e−2t

5. Invert, then shift by 3

y = e−2t + u3(t)(2 − 2e−2(t − 3))

The first term comes from y(0). The second term comes from the force, and it starts at t = 3.

Example 2 — a spring, with a force switched on at t = 1

y″ + 4y = 4u1(t),   y(0) = 0,  y′(0) = 0

2. Transform both sides

s2Y + 4Y = 4e−ss

3. Solve for Y

Y = e−s4s(s2 + 4)

4. Partial fractions, without e−s

4s(s2 + 4) = 1s − ss2 + 4  →  1 − cos 2t

5. Invert, then shift by 1

y = u1(t)(1 − cos 2(t − 1))

This is Friday’s Example 8. Today we see where such a Y(s) comes from.

The constant force 4 moves the rest position from 0 to 4/4 = 1. With no damping, the spring keeps swinging around it.

↳ how to check this answer.

Checking Example 2, piece by piece

y = 0 for t < 1,   y = 1 − cos 2(t − 1) for t ≥ 1

On each piece

t < 1:  0 + 0 = 0 ✓

t ≥ 1:  y″ = 4 cos 2(t − 1)

y″ + 4y = 4 cos 2(t − 1) + 4 − 4 cos 2(t − 1) = 4 ✓

At the switch t = 1

y: 0 and 1 − cos 0 = 0 ✓

y′: 0 and 2 sin 0 = 0 ✓

y″: 0 and 4, a jump of 4

Since y″ = g − 4y, y″ jumps exactly as much as g jumps. y and y′ stay continuous.

↳ the same spring, not starting at rest.

Example 3 — the same spring, with y(0) = 1

The initial values give the terms without e−cs. The force gives the terms with e−cs. Keep the two groups apart.

Example 4 — a force that switches off

y″ + y = 1 − uπ(t),   y(0) = 0,  y′(0) = 0

2–3. Transform, solve for Y

(s2 + 1)Y = 1s − e−πss

Y = 1s(s2 + 1) − e−πs1s(s2 + 1)

4. One fraction, used twice

1s(s2 + 1) = 1s − ss2 + 1  →  1 − cos t

5. Invert, shift the second term by π

y = 1 − cos t − uπ(t)(1 − cos(t − π))

For t ≥ π

cos(t − π) = −cos t,  so  y = 1 − cos t − 1 − cos t = −2 cos t

↳ the picture.

t ≥ π:  y = −2 cos t

At t = π the mass is at y = 2 with y′ = 0. The force stops, and it swings between 2 and −2 from there.

Example 6 — the force must be rewritten first

y″ + y = tu2(t),   y(0) = 0,  y′(0) = 0

1. Rewrite (Friday): g(t + 2) = t + 2

ℒ{tu2(t)} = e−2s(1s2 + 2s)

3. Solve for Y

Y = e−2s(1s2(s2 + 1) + 2s(s2 + 1))

4. Partial fractions, one at a time

1s2(s2 + 1) = 1s2 − 1s2 + 1  →  t − sin t

2s(s2 + 1) = 2s − 2ss2 + 1  →  2 − 2 cos t

5. Add, then shift by 2

y = u2(t)((t − 2) − sin(t − 2) + 2 − 2 cos(t − 2))

= u2(t)(t − sin(t − 2) − 2 cos(t − 2));  at t = 2: 2 − 0 − 2 = 0 ✓

↳ a force that is on for a while.

Example 7 — on from π to 2π

The card — equations with a switching force

Method

partial fractions without e−cs, invert, shift

What to expect

initial values → terms with no shift

each switch at c → a term uc(t)(…)
